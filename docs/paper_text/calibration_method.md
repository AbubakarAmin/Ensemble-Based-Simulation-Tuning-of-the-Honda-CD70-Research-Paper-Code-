### 3.5 Calibration by approximate Bayesian rejection

Because no measurements of the target engine are available, we do not fit one set of parameters. Instead we sample the 38 uncertain inputs from their prior ranges with a scrambled Sobol sequence, simulate each candidate engine, and keep every candidate whose simulated behaviour satisfies all calibration conditions of Table @tab:cal@ simultaneously. This is rejection approximate Bayesian computation [R16] with a hard acceptance window, so the accepted candidates form an ensemble of engines that are each consistent with the published data. Sampling proceeds in rounds of 32,768 candidates (up to eight rounds) until at least 100 engines are accepted; a run that fails to reach 100 is reported as such and no tolerance is relaxed. The calibration conditions combine the two published figures (power and torque), the older workshop-manual cranking pressure, a real-world top-speed range, and plausibility windows (specific fuel consumption, volumetric efficiency, crank angle of peak pressure and peak cylinder pressure).

The cranking pressure target needs a note. The workshop manual gives 12.7 bar at 1,000 rpm, but a kick-start or starter motor does not turn the engine at 1,000 rpm, and a healthy engine does not reach 12.7 bar at that speed. We therefore treat the test speed (300-1,000 rpm) and the blow-by area as uncertain and let the calibration data identify the combinations that are consistent with the published pressure.

### 3.6 Ensemble, nominal engine and validity pass

The accepted candidates are re-evaluated with the design rules and plausibility checks of Section 3.3 (the validity pass), so that only engines that are physically credible in their baseline state remain. The accepted engine closest to the posterior median of the standardised inputs is used as the nominal engine for screening and for the single-engine optimisers; every robustness and comparison result uses the whole ensemble, and the two readings of the workshop valve timing (A: as printed; B: 20 degrees added to each flank) are calibrated and analysed separately. How strongly the data constrain each input is summarised by the ratio of posterior to prior standard deviation, and the dependence among accepted inputs is summarised by rank correlations and a principal component analysis.
